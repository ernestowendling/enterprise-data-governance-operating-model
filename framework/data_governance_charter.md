# Enterprise Data Governance Charter

## 1. Purpose

This charter establishes the mandate, scope, principles, accountability model and decision structure for enterprise Data Governance at HelvetiaCare Insurance Group.

The Data Governance programme enables data to be managed as a trusted enterprise asset and used responsibly for:

- Business operations
- Customer and provider services
- Management decision-making
- Regulatory and financial reporting
- Analytics
- Process automation
- Artificial intelligence

The charter translates strategic governance objectives into clear responsibilities, decision rights, standards and oversight mechanisms.

## 2. Objectives

The Data Governance programme aims to:

1. Establish clear accountability for enterprise data.
2. Define consistent business terminology and data standards.
3. Identify and govern critical data elements.
4. Improve data quality, availability and trustworthiness.
5. Ensure that data risks are identified, assessed and managed.
6. Embed governance requirements into business processes and technology.
7. Support compliant and responsible use of sensitive information.
8. Enable trustworthy analytics, automation and artificial intelligence.
9. Produce measurable evidence that governance controls operate effectively.
10. Promote continuous improvement in enterprise Data Management maturity.

## 3. Scope

This charter applies to:

- Structured and unstructured enterprise data
- Data created, received, processed, stored or shared by HelvetiaCare
- Data managed through internal systems or external service providers
- Business, operational, financial, customer and health-related information
- Data used for reporting, analytics, automation and artificial intelligence
- Employees, contractors and service providers who create or manage data

The initial implementation covers the following data domains:

1. Customer
2. Policy
3. Claims
4. Healthcare Provider
5. Finance

Additional domains may be added following approval by the Data Governance Council.

## 4. Governance Principles

HelvetiaCare applies the following principles:

### 4.1 Business accountability

Accountability for data remains with the business functions that define, create and use it.

### 4.2 Clear ownership

Critical data must have an assigned Data Owner and an operational Data Steward.

### 4.3 Common definitions

Important business terms must have clear, approved and accessible definitions.

### 4.4 Proportionate controls

Governance controls must reflect the sensitivity, criticality and business impact of the data.

### 4.5 Data quality by design

Data-quality requirements must be defined when processes, systems and products are designed or changed.

### 4.6 Controlled access

Access to data must be authorised according to business need, role and data classification.

### 4.7 Traceability

Material data transformations, decisions, exceptions and governance actions must be documented and auditable.

### 4.8 Responsible usage

Data must be used consistently with approved purposes, applicable requirements and customer expectations.

### 4.9 Issue ownership

Data issues must have a responsible owner, a documented remediation plan and an agreed resolution target.

### 4.10 Continuous improvement

Governance effectiveness must be measured through indicators, maturity assessments and periodic reviews.

## 5. Governance Organisation

### 5.1 Executive sponsorship

The Chief Data Officer acts as executive sponsor of the enterprise Data Governance programme.

The executive sponsor:

- Promotes enterprise-wide adoption
- Secures appropriate resources
- Resolves material cross-functional conflicts
- Escalates strategic matters to executive management
- Ensures alignment with the Data and AI strategy

### 5.2 Data Governance Council

The Data Governance Council is the principal cross-functional decision body for enterprise Data Governance.

Its membership includes representatives from:

- Chief Data Office
- Business data domains
- Data Architecture
- Data and AI Centre of Excellence
- Data Protection
- Compliance
- Information Security
- Risk Management
- Technology
- Internal Control functions

The Council:

- Approves governance principles and standards
- Confirms domain ownership
- Resolves cross-domain issues
- Reviews governance performance
- Approves material exceptions
- Prioritises enterprise remediation initiatives
- Reviews Data Governance maturity
- Escalates unresolved material risks

### 5.3 Data Owners

Data Owners are senior business representatives accountable for data within an assigned domain.

They are accountable for:

- Approving business definitions
- Establishing data-quality expectations
- Confirming critical data elements
- Approving access and usage principles
- Sponsoring issue remediation
- Accepting residual data risks within their authority
- Reporting governance performance

### 5.4 Data Stewards

Data Stewards coordinate the operational implementation of governance within their domains.

They are responsible for:

- Maintaining metadata and business definitions
- Monitoring data-quality indicators
- Recording and assessing data issues
- Coordinating remediation activities
- Supporting governance reporting
- Facilitating domain-level governance meetings
- Escalating overdue or high-risk issues

### 5.5 Data Custodians

Data Custodians implement and operate technical controls for systems and data platforms.

They are responsible for:

- Technical data storage and processing
- Access-control implementation
- System-level validation
- Backup and recovery controls
- Technical metadata
- Data transfer and integration controls
- Evidence supporting control execution

### 5.6 Control and advisory functions

Data Protection, Compliance, Information Security, Risk Management and Architecture provide specialist review and challenge.

They do not replace the accountability of Data Owners.

## 6. Decision Rights

The following decision principles apply:

- Enterprise governance policies are approved by the Data Governance Council.
- Domain definitions are approved by the relevant Data Owner.
- Critical data elements are proposed by Data Stewards and approved by Data Owners.
- Technical controls are designed with Data Custodians and relevant control functions.
- Material cross-domain conflicts are decided by the Data Governance Council.
- Material exceptions must have an owner, rationale, expiry date and remediation plan.
- Risks exceeding delegated authority are escalated to executive management.

Detailed decision rights are documented separately in the Decision Rights Framework.

## 8. Governance Lifecycle

Governance requirements are implemented through the following lifecycle:

1. Identify the data asset or business requirement.
2. Assign ownership and stewardship.
3. Define business meaning and criticality.
4. Classify the data.
5. Establish quality and usage requirements.
6. Translate requirements into processes and controls.
7. Monitor control performance.
8. Record and remediate issues.
9. Escalate unresolved risks and exceptions.
10. Review effectiveness and improve the framework.

## 9. Issue and Escalation Management

Data issues must be recorded when they could affect:

- Customers
- Business operations
- Financial or regulatory reporting
- Compliance
- Information security
- Data protection
- Analytics or artificial intelligence
- Management decisions

Each issue must include:

- Affected data domain
- Description and business impact
- Severity
- Responsible Data Steward
- Accountable Data Owner
- Remediation action
- Target resolution date
- Current status
- Supporting evidence

High-severity and overdue issues are escalated according to the Data Issue Management Standard.

## 10. Exceptions

Exceptions to governance standards must be:

- Explicitly documented
- Supported by a business rationale
- Assessed for risk
- Approved by the appropriate authority
- Assigned an expiry date
- Supported by compensating controls where required
- Periodically reviewed
- Closed when the underlying condition no longer exists

Permanent undocumented exceptions are not permitted.

## 11. Performance Measurement

Governance effectiveness is measured through indicators including:

- Ownership coverage
- Stewardship coverage
- Business-glossary completeness
- Critical data-element coverage
- Data-quality performance
- Number and severity of open issues
- Issue-resolution time
- Overdue remediation actions
- Policy-exception volume
- Training completion
- Governance maturity scores
- AI data-readiness assessments

Targets are reviewed periodically by the Data Governance Council.

## 12. Review and Maintenance

This charter is reviewed at least annually or following a material change to:

- Business strategy
- Regulatory requirements
- Organisational structure
- Technology architecture
- Data or AI strategy
- Enterprise risk exposure

The Chief Data Office coordinates the review.

Material amendments require approval from the Data Governance Council.

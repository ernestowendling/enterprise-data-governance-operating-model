# Enterprise Data Governance Principles

## Purpose

These principles define the expected behaviours and decision criteria for managing data at HelvetiaCare Insurance Group.

They apply across business functions, data domains, systems, projects, analytics, automation and artificial intelligence initiatives.

The principles complement the Enterprise Data Governance Charter and guide the development of policies, standards, controls and processes.

---

## Principle 1 — Data is an enterprise asset

Data must be managed according to its value, criticality and risk to the organisation.

### Expected behaviours

- Data is managed across organisational boundaries.
- Important data is not treated as the exclusive property of one team.
- Decisions consider enterprise-wide impacts.
- Reuse is encouraged where legally and operationally appropriate.
- Critical data assets are documented and governed.

### Evidence

- Approved data-domain register
- Critical data-element inventory
- Data ownership assignments
- Enterprise metadata records

---

## Principle 2 — Business accountability is explicit

Business functions remain accountable for the meaning, quality and appropriate use of their data.

Technology teams support implementation but do not replace business accountability.

### Expected behaviours

- Every data domain has an accountable Data Owner.
- Critical data elements have assigned Data Stewards.
- Accountability is documented and communicated.
- Decisions are made at the appropriate organisational level.
- Ownership gaps are escalated.

### Evidence

- Data ownership register
- Stewardship assignments
- RACI matrix
- Governance decision records

---

## Principle 3 — Data has a common business meaning

Important data must be defined consistently and understood by the people who create, manage and use it.

### Expected behaviours

- Business terms have approved definitions.
- Synonyms and conflicting definitions are identified.
- Definitions include ownership and domain information.
- Changes follow an approval workflow.
- Retired definitions remain traceable.

### Evidence

- Business glossary
- Definition approval history
- Metadata completeness reports
- Change records

---

## Principle 4 — Governance is proportionate to risk

The level of governance must reflect data sensitivity, business criticality and potential impact.

### Expected behaviours

- Sensitive and critical data receives stronger controls.
- Low-risk data is not subject to unnecessary complexity.
- Control requirements are based on documented risk.
- Exceptions include compensating measures where appropriate.
- Governance requirements are periodically reassessed.

### Evidence

- Data classification records
- Risk assessments
- Control mappings
- Exception approvals

---

## Principle 5 — Data quality is defined by business need

Data quality must be assessed against clear business and regulatory requirements.

### Expected behaviours

- Quality requirements are defined by Data Owners.
- Critical data elements have measurable quality rules.
- Thresholds reflect business impact.
- Failures are recorded and assigned for remediation.
- Persistent issues are escalated.

### Evidence

- Data-quality rules
- Quality dashboards
- Issue records
- Remediation plans
- Threshold approvals

---

## Principle 6 — Data quality is built into processes

Quality must be considered when processes, products, systems and interfaces are designed or changed.

### Expected behaviours

- Validation is implemented as close as possible to data creation.
- Preventive controls are preferred over retrospective correction.
- Change initiatives assess data-quality impacts.
- System interfaces include reconciliation controls.
- Material control changes are tested.

### Evidence

- Design assessments
- Validation rules
- Reconciliation reports
- Test evidence
- Change approvals

---

## Principle 7 — Access is based on legitimate need

Access to data must be authorised, limited and periodically reviewed.

### Expected behaviours

- Access is granted according to role and business purpose.
- Sensitive data receives enhanced protection.
- Privileged access is controlled.
- Access rights are reviewed periodically.
- Inappropriate access is removed promptly.

### Evidence

- Access approvals
- Role definitions
- Access-review records
- Privileged-access logs
- Revocation evidence

---

## Principle 8 — Data usage must be transparent

The organisation must understand how important data is collected, transformed, shared and used.

### Expected behaviours

- Material data flows are documented.
- Source systems are identified.
- Transformations affecting meaning are traceable.
- Data consumers understand relevant limitations.
- Automated and AI-supported decisions are explainable at an appropriate level.

### Evidence

- Data-flow diagrams
- Lineage records
- Transformation documentation
- Model or AI use-case assessments
- Data usage records

---

## Principle 9 — Sensitive data is protected throughout its lifecycle

Protection requirements apply from data creation or receipt through use, retention and disposal.

### Expected behaviours

- Data is classified when created or onboarded.
- Protection follows the classification level.
- Retention periods are defined.
- Data is disposed of securely.
- Third-party processing is governed.

### Evidence

- Classification records
- Retention schedules
- Disposal records
- Supplier-control evidence
- Security assessments

---

## Principle 10 — Governance decisions are auditable

Material decisions, approvals, exceptions and remediation actions must leave sufficient evidence.

### Expected behaviours

- Decisions include an accountable approver.
- Rationales are documented.
- Changes are version-controlled.
- Exceptions have expiry dates.
- Evidence is retained according to applicable requirements.

### Evidence

- Decision logs
- Approval records
- Version history
- Exception register
- Audit trail

---

## Principle 11 — Data issues are actively managed

Data issues must be assessed according to their impact and resolved within agreed timelines.

### Expected behaviours

- Issues are recorded consistently.
- Severity is based on defined criteria.
- Each issue has a responsible person.
- Remediation actions and deadlines are documented.
- Overdue and high-severity issues are escalated.

### Evidence

- Data issue register
- Severity assessments
- Remediation plans
- Escalation records
- Closure evidence

---

## Principle 12 — Governance is embedded in delivery

Data Governance must be integrated into business change, technology delivery and operational processes.

### Expected behaviours

- Projects assess governance impacts.
- New systems identify ownership and critical data.
- Governance requirements appear in delivery criteria.
- Controls are implemented before production use.
- Operational teams receive appropriate guidance.

### Evidence

- Project governance assessments
- Data-readiness reviews
- Acceptance criteria
- Control implementation evidence
- Training records

---

## Principle 13 — Data supports responsible analytics and AI

Analytics and artificial intelligence must use data that is sufficiently governed for the intended purpose.

### Expected behaviours

- Data sources and limitations are documented.
- Quality is assessed before use.
- Sensitive data usage is reviewed.
- Ownership and accountability remain clear.
- Outputs are monitored for material data-related risks.
- Human oversight is applied where appropriate.

### Evidence

- AI data-readiness assessments
- Data-source inventories
- Quality reports
- Use-case approvals
- Monitoring records

---

## Principle 14 — Governance performance is measurable

The organisation must assess whether Data Governance is operating effectively.

### Expected behaviours

- Governance KPIs have clear definitions.
- Targets are approved.
- Results are reviewed regularly.
- Material weaknesses result in improvement actions.
- Maturity is reassessed periodically.

### Evidence

- Governance KPI dashboard
- Data-domain scorecards
- Maturity assessments
- Improvement plans
- Council meeting records

---

## Principle 15 — Governance improves continuously

The governance model must evolve with business strategy, regulation, technology and risk.

### Expected behaviours

- Policies and standards are reviewed periodically.
- Lessons from incidents and issues are incorporated.
- Stakeholder feedback is considered.
- Governance processes are simplified where possible.
- Automation is introduced where it improves consistency and evidence.

### Evidence

- Review schedules
- Updated standards
- Lessons-learned records
- Process improvement backlog
- Automation control records

---

## Application of the Principles

The principles must be considered when:

- Creating or changing a business process
- Implementing or replacing a system
- Introducing a new data source
- Sharing data internally or externally
- Defining a data product
- Designing reporting or analytics
- Developing automation or artificial intelligence
- Approving a governance exception
- Assessing a material data issue

Where principles conflict, the matter must be escalated to the relevant Data Owner or the Data Governance Council.

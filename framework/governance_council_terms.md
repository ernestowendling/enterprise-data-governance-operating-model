# Data Governance Council Terms of Reference

## 1. Purpose

These Terms of Reference define the mandate, authority, membership, responsibilities and operating procedures of the HelvetiaCare Insurance Group Data Governance Council.

The Council provides enterprise oversight of Data Governance and supports the responsible, consistent and value-generating use of data across the organisation.

The Council operates under the authority of the Chief Data Officer and within the framework established by the Enterprise Data Governance Charter.

---

## 2. Mandate

The Data Governance Council is the principal cross-functional decision body for enterprise Data Governance.

Its mandate is to:

- Establish consistent enterprise governance
- Approve governance policies and standards
- Confirm data ownership and accountability
- Resolve cross-domain matters
- Review governance performance
- Oversee material data risks and issues
- Approve material governance exceptions
- Prioritise remediation and improvement initiatives
- Support trustworthy analytics, automation and artificial intelligence
- Escalate matters beyond its delegated authority

The Council does not replace the accountability of Data Owners or the responsibilities of control functions.

---

## 3. Objectives

The Council aims to:

1. Ensure that enterprise data has clear ownership.
2. Promote consistent definitions and standards.
3. Improve data quality and trustworthiness.
4. Support compliant and responsible data usage.
5. Resolve material cross-domain conflicts.
6. Ensure that governance requirements are operationalised.
7. Review the effectiveness of governance controls.
8. Promote measurable improvement in Data Management maturity.
9. Support responsible analytics and artificial intelligence.
10. Maintain an auditable record of material governance decisions.

---

## 4. Authority

The Data Governance Council has authority to:

- Approve enterprise Data Governance standards
- Confirm Data Owner appointments
- Resolve ownership disputes
- Approve enterprise business definitions
- Resolve cross-domain definition conflicts
- Approve material governance exceptions
- Prioritise enterprise remediation initiatives
- Require corrective actions
- Request additional governance evidence
- Escalate material risks to executive management
- Request participation from relevant stakeholders
- Establish working groups for specific matters

The Council may delegate defined activities but retains oversight of delegated responsibilities.

---

## 5. Scope

The Council's scope includes:

- Enterprise data domains
- Data ownership and stewardship
- Business definitions and metadata
- Critical data elements
- Data quality
- Data classification
- Data access and usage
- Data issue management
- Governance exceptions
- Data lifecycle management
- Governance tooling
- Governance KPIs
- Maturity assessments
- Data readiness for analytics and AI
- Cross-domain data dependencies
- Material third-party data arrangements

The Council does not replace:

- Executive Management
- Data Protection governance
- Information Security governance
- Compliance governance
- Risk Management
- Internal Audit
- Technology governance
- Project governance

The Council coordinates with these functions where responsibilities overlap.

---

## 6. Membership

### 6.1 Chair

The Council is chaired by:

- The Chief Data Officer; or
- A formally appointed delegate

The Chair is accountable for the effective operation of the Council.

### 6.2 Permanent members

Permanent membership includes representatives from:

- Chief Data Office
- Customer data domain
- Policy data domain
- Claims data domain
- Healthcare Provider data domain
- Finance data domain
- Data Architecture
- Enterprise Architecture
- Data and AI Centre of Excellence
- Data Protection
- Compliance
- Information Security
- Risk Management
- Technology

### 6.3 Optional members

The Chair may invite representatives from:

- Legal
- Internal Control
- Internal Audit
- Human Resources
- Procurement
- Product Management
- Business Operations
- Analytics teams
- Artificial intelligence teams
- External service-provider management
- Programme or project teams

Optional members participate where their expertise is relevant.

### 6.4 Member seniority

Members must have sufficient authority to:

- Represent their function
- Make or recommend decisions
- Commit resources where appropriate
- Accept actions
- Escalate matters within their organisation

### 6.5 Delegates

Permanent members may appoint delegates where:

- The delegate has sufficient subject-matter knowledge
- The delegate has appropriate decision authority
- The delegation is communicated before the meeting
- Continuity of representation is maintained

---

## 7. Roles

### 7.1 Chair

The Chair:

- Approves the meeting agenda
- Ensures that priorities are addressed
- Facilitates decision-making
- Confirms decisions and actions
- Resolves procedural disputes
- Escalates matters beyond Council authority
- Ensures alignment with enterprise strategy
- Reviews Council effectiveness

### 7.2 Secretariat

The Chief Data Office provides the Council Secretariat.

The Secretariat:

- Schedules meetings
- Prepares agendas
- Coordinates meeting materials
- Maintains the decision log
- Records minutes and actions
- Tracks overdue actions
- Maintains the exception register
- Retains governance evidence
- Supports reporting to executive management

### 7.3 Members

Council members:

- Review materials before meetings
- Represent their functions and domains
- Provide informed advice
- Participate in decisions
- Declare conflicts of interest
- Accept and complete assigned actions
- Escalate matters within their areas
- Communicate Council decisions to relevant stakeholders

### 7.4 Data Owners

Data Owners participating in the Council:

- Present domain performance
- Escalate material issues
- Request cross-domain decisions
- Sponsor remediation initiatives
- Report on overdue actions
- Confirm implementation of Council decisions

### 7.5 Control-function representatives

Control-function representatives:

- Provide independent advice and challenge
- Identify regulatory or control implications
- Recommend conditions and safeguards
- Escalate matters within their mandates
- Confirm whether specialist review is required

---

## 8. Responsibilities

The Council is responsible for the following areas.

### 8.1 Governance framework

The Council:

- Approves the Data Governance Charter
- Approves governance principles
- Approves enterprise standards
- Reviews framework effectiveness
- Requires updates where material gaps exist

### 8.2 Ownership and accountability

The Council:

- Confirms enterprise Data Owner appointments
- Resolves ownership disputes
- Reviews ownership coverage
- Requires remediation of accountability gaps
- Reviews cross-domain responsibilities

### 8.3 Business definitions and metadata

The Council:

- Resolves cross-domain definition conflicts
- Approves enterprise terms where required
- Reviews glossary adoption
- Supports consistent metadata practices
- Requires remediation of material definition gaps

### 8.4 Data quality

The Council:

- Reviews enterprise data-quality performance
- Reviews material quality failures
- Challenges inadequate remediation
- Approves enterprise quality priorities
- Reviews persistent threshold breaches

### 8.5 Data issues

The Council:

- Reviews high-severity and overdue issues
- Confirms escalation decisions
- Prioritises cross-domain remediation
- Requires accountable owners and deadlines
- Reviews issue closure where material risk exists

### 8.6 Governance exceptions

The Council:

- Reviews material exception requests
- Assesses the business rationale
- Reviews risk and compensating controls
- Approves, rejects or conditions exceptions
- Ensures that exceptions have expiry dates
- Reviews overdue or repeatedly renewed exceptions

### 8.7 Governance performance

The Council:

- Reviews governance KPIs
- Reviews maturity-assessment results
- Approves improvement priorities
- Monitors implementation progress
- Challenges poor or deteriorating performance

### 8.8 Data and AI enablement

The Council:

- Reviews material data-readiness concerns
- Supports responsible analytics and AI
- Escalates material data-related AI risks
- Confirms ownership for AI-related data
- Requires appropriate governance evidence before material use

---

## 9. Meeting Cadence

The Council meets monthly.

Additional meetings may be scheduled when:

- A material incident occurs
- An urgent governance decision is required
- A regulatory or customer impact is possible
- A significant exception requires review
- An executive escalation is necessary
- A major data or AI initiative requires timely assessment

The Chair may cancel a meeting where no material agenda items exist.

---

## 10. Agenda

The standard agenda includes:

1. Approval of the previous meeting minutes
2. Review of outstanding actions
3. Review of governance KPIs
4. Review of high-severity and overdue data issues
5. Review of governance exceptions
6. Cross-domain decisions
7. Policy and standard approvals
8. Maturity and improvement initiatives
9. Data and AI governance matters
10. Escalations to executive management
11. Any other business

The agenda may be adjusted according to business priorities.

---

## 11. Meeting Materials

Meeting materials should be distributed at least three working days before the meeting.

Materials may include:

- Decision papers
- KPI dashboards
- Issue reports
- Exception requests
- Risk assessments
- Draft standards
- Maturity reports
- Remediation proposals
- Data and AI readiness assessments
- Architecture or control assessments

Late papers may be accepted at the Chair's discretion.

---

## 12. Decision Papers

A decision paper should contain:

- Decision title
- Decision requested
- Relevant data domain
- Business context
- Current situation
- Options considered
- Recommended option
- Risks
- Regulatory and control implications
- Financial or resource implications
- Stakeholders consulted
- Required actions
- Proposed decision authority
- Supporting evidence

Incomplete decision papers may be returned for further analysis.

---

## 13. Quorum

The Council is quorate when:

- The Chair or appointed delegate is present
- At least half of permanent members are present
- The relevant Data Owner is represented for a domain-specific decision
- Relevant control functions are represented where their mandates are materially affected

A decision may be deferred where essential expertise or authority is absent.

---

## 14. Decision-Making

The Council aims to make decisions by consensus.

Where consensus cannot be reached:

1. The Chair summarises the areas of agreement and disagreement.
2. Relevant risks and objections are documented.
3. The Chair may call for a formal vote.
4. Each permanent member has one vote.
5. A simple majority determines the decision.
6. The Chair has the casting vote where votes are equal.
7. Material unresolved concerns may be escalated to executive management.

Control functions retain their independent escalation rights.

---

## 15. Written Decisions

The Council may make a written decision outside a scheduled meeting where:

- The matter is urgent
- Sufficient evidence is available
- Relevant members have been consulted
- Quorum requirements can be demonstrated
- The decision is recorded in the decision log

Written decisions must be presented at the next Council meeting for formal acknowledgement.

---

## 16. Conflicts of Interest

Members must declare actual or potential conflicts of interest.

Where a conflict exists:

- The conflict is recorded
- The Chair determines the appropriate participation level
- The member may provide factual information
- The member may be excluded from the final decision
- The decision record documents how the conflict was managed

---

## 17. Minutes

Meeting minutes must include:

- Date and time
- Attendees
- Apologies
- Conflicts of interest
- Agenda items
- Key discussion points
- Decisions
- Decision rationales
- Conditions
- Actions
- Action owners
- Target dates
- Escalations
- Supporting documents

Minutes are approved at the next meeting or through an agreed written process.

---

## 18. Action Management

Each action must include:

- Action identifier
- Description
- Accountable owner
- Target completion date
- Status
- Dependencies
- Completion evidence

Action statuses are:

- Open
- In progress
- Blocked
- Completed
- Closed
- Overdue
- Cancelled

Overdue material actions are reviewed at each Council meeting.

---

## 19. Escalation

The Council escalates a matter to executive management where:

- Risk exceeds delegated authority
- Significant investment is required
- A material issue remains unresolved
- Regulatory notification may be required
- Significant customer impact may occur
- Enterprise strategy is affected
- A Data Owner or business function refuses required remediation
- A control function raises an unresolved material concern

The escalation must include:

- Clear description of the matter
- Business and risk impact
- Options considered
- Council recommendation
- Required executive decision
- Proposed actions and timelines

---

## 20. Confidentiality

Council materials must be handled according to their classification.

Members must:

- Use information only for authorised purposes
- Protect sensitive information
- Avoid unnecessary distribution
- Follow document-retention requirements
- Report suspected unauthorised disclosure

---

## 21. Records and Retention

The Secretariat maintains:

- Agendas
- Meeting materials
- Attendance records
- Minutes
- Decision records
- Action logs
- Exception records
- Escalation records
- Supporting evidence

Records must remain:

- Complete
- Accurate
- Traceable
- Protected from unauthorised alteration
- Accessible for governance review and audit
- Retained according to applicable requirements

---

## 22. Council Performance

The effectiveness of the Council may be measured through:

- Meeting attendance
- Percentage of decisions completed within target
- Percentage of actions completed within target
- Number of overdue material actions
- Average decision cycle time
- Number of repeated escalations
- Number of expired exceptions
- Stakeholder feedback
- Governance KPI improvement
- Maturity-assessment progress

The Council reviews its effectiveness at least annually.

---

## 23. Annual Review

The Terms of Reference are reviewed annually or following a material change to:

- Enterprise strategy
- Governance structure
- Regulatory requirements
- Data and AI strategy
- Organisational responsibilities
- Risk exposure

Material amendments require Council approval.

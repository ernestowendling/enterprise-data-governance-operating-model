# Organisation Context

## 1. Organisation Overview

HelvetiaCare Insurance Group is a fictional Swiss health-insurance organisation created exclusively for this portfolio project.

The organisation provides insurance products and healthcare-related services to individual and corporate customers across Switzerland.

All organisations, people, systems, processes and datasets referenced in this repository are fictional or synthetic.

---

## 2. Business Model

HelvetiaCare uses data to support:

- Customer onboarding and servicing
- Insurance-policy administration
- Claims processing
- Healthcare-provider management
- Premium collection
- Financial reporting
- Regulatory reporting
- Business analytics
- Process automation
- Artificial intelligence use cases

The organisation is expanding its use of data, analytics and AI to improve operational efficiency, customer service and decision-making.

This creates a need for a consistent enterprise Data Governance framework.

## 4. Strategic Data Context

HelvetiaCare aims to use data as a trusted enterprise asset.

Its Data and AI strategy includes:

- Improving the quality of operational data
- Creating consistent enterprise definitions
- Strengthening data ownership and stewardship
- Increasing transparency over data lineage
- Improving access to trusted data
- Enabling responsible analytics
- Automating repetitive business processes
- Supporting responsible artificial intelligence
- Improving regulatory and management reporting
- Creating measurable Data Governance maturity

The organisation recognises that advanced analytics and AI depend on reliable, well-governed and appropriately controlled data.

---

## 5. Current Governance Challenges

Data responsibilities have historically been distributed across business units and technology teams.

This has resulted in several challenges:

- Inconsistent business definitions
- Unclear ownership and accountability
- Different data-quality practices across departments
- Limited visibility over critical data elements
- Fragmented issue-management processes
- Incomplete metadata
- Limited data-lineage documentation
- Inconsistent access-review practices
- Limited evidence that governance requirements are operationalised
- Weak measurement of governance effectiveness
- Unclear data-readiness criteria for analytics and AI

These challenges reduce confidence in enterprise data and increase operational, regulatory and reputational risk.

---

## 6. Data Governance Programme

HelvetiaCare has established an enterprise Data Governance programme sponsored by the Chief Data Officer.

The programme aims to:

1. Establish clear ownership and stewardship responsibilities.
2. Create common governance principles and standards.
3. Identify and govern critical data elements.
4. Improve data quality and metadata completeness.
5. Define transparent decision and escalation processes.
6. Integrate governance requirements into operational processes and tools.
7. Support responsible analytics, automation and artificial intelligence.
8. Measure governance effectiveness through KPIs and maturity assessments.
9. Improve collaboration between business, technology and control functions.
10. Produce auditable evidence of governance decisions and controls.

---

## 7. Governance Operating Model

HelvetiaCare uses a federated Data Governance operating model.

Under this model:

- The Chief Data Office defines the enterprise framework.
- The Data Governance Council approves enterprise standards and decisions.
- Business Data Owners retain accountability for their domains.
- Data Stewards coordinate operational governance activities.
- Data Custodians implement and operate technical controls.
- Control functions provide advice and independent challenge.
- The Data and AI Centre of Excellence supports tooling, automation and responsible AI.
- Business and technology teams apply governance requirements in daily work.

This structure combines enterprise consistency with decentralised business ownership.

## 8. Initial Data Governance Scope

The initial governance scope covers five enterprise data domains:

1. Customer
2. Policy
3. Claims
4. Healthcare Provider
5. Finance

Each domain has:

- An accountable Data Owner
- An operational Data Steward
- Relevant Data Custodians
- Defined critical data elements
- Identified business risks
- Governance controls
- Governance KPIs
- Related governance standards

Additional data domains may be introduced as the governance programme matures.

## 9. Data Domains

### 9.1 Customer

The Customer domain contains data used to identify, onboard, communicate with and provide services to customers.

Examples include:

- Customer identifiers
- Legal names
- Dates of birth
- Addresses
- Contact information
- Consent status
- Customer relationship status

The accountable role is the Head of Customer Management.

---

### 9.2 Policy

The Policy domain contains data describing insurance contracts, coverage conditions and contractual relationships.

Examples include:

- Policy identifiers
- Policyholder identifiers
- Coverage type
- Policy status
- Inception dates
- Expiry dates
- Premium information

The accountable role is the Head of Policy Administration.

---

### 9.3 Claims

The Claims domain contains data used to register, assess, process and settle insurance claims.

Examples include:

- Claim identifiers
- Policy references
- Submission dates
- Treatment dates
- Claim amounts
- Reimbursement amounts
- Claim status

The accountable role is the Head of Claims Management.

---

### 9.4 Healthcare Provider

The Healthcare Provider domain contains data describing healthcare professionals and organisations involved in insurance and reimbursement processes.

Examples include:

- Provider identifiers
- Provider legal names
- Provider types
- Licence status
- Contract status
- Payment details

The accountable role is the Head of Provider Management.

---

### 9.5 Finance

The Finance domain contains data used for accounting, payment processing, financial reporting and regulatory reporting.

Examples include:

- Transaction identifiers
- Accounting dates
- Transaction amounts
- Currency codes
- General ledger accounts
- Payment status
- Reporting periods

The accountable role is the Chief Financial Officer.

## 14. Governance Success Measures

The Data Governance programme will be considered effective when:

- All data domains have approved Data Owners
- Critical data elements have assigned Data Stewards
- Business definitions are approved and accessible
- Data-quality rules are measurable
- High-severity issues are escalated promptly
- Governance actions are completed within target
- Metadata completeness improves
- Governance exceptions are documented and time-limited
- Data and AI use cases complete appropriate readiness assessments
- Governance maturity improves over time
- Material decisions and controls remain auditable

# Data Owner Role Profile

## 1. Purpose

The Data Owner is the senior business role accountable for the definition, quality, appropriate use, protection and governance of data within an assigned enterprise data domain.

The Data Owner ensures that data is managed as a trusted business asset and that governance requirements are translated into practical decisions, controls and remediation priorities.

The role forms part of the federated Data Governance operating model of HelvetiaCare Insurance Group.

---

## 2. Role Positioning

The Data Owner represents the business function that creates, manages or relies upon the data within an assigned domain.

The Data Owner:

- Retains business accountability for the domain
- Provides strategic direction
- Approves material governance decisions
- Sponsors remediation
- Resolves domain-level conflicts
- Escalates matters beyond delegated authority
- Represents the domain in enterprise governance forums

Technology teams, Data Stewards and Data Custodians support the Data Owner but do not replace business accountability.

---

## 3. Appointment

A Data Owner must:

- Hold sufficient seniority within the relevant business function
- Understand the business processes supported by the domain
- Possess authority to approve standards, priorities and remediation
- Be able to commit appropriate business resources
- Understand the principal regulatory, operational and customer risks
- Participate in enterprise Data Governance decision-making

Data Owner appointments are proposed by the Chief Data Office and confirmed by the Data Governance Council.

---

## 4. Core Accountabilities

The Data Owner is accountable for the following areas.

### 4.1 Business meaning

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Ensuring that important data has a clear business meaning
- Approving domain business definitions
- Resolving conflicting definitions within the domain
- Supporting resolution of cross-domain definition conflicts
- Ensuring that terminology remains aligned with business processes

### 4.2 Data ownership

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Confirming the scope of the assigned domain
- Ensuring that critical data elements have named accountability
- Identifying ownership gaps
- Escalating disputed or shared ownership
- Supporting clear allocation of cross-domain responsibilities

### 4.3 Data quality

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Approving data-quality expectations
- Approving quality dimensions and thresholds
- Confirming which data-quality failures are material
- Reviewing domain quality performance
- Sponsoring remediation of material quality issues
- Accepting temporary quality limitations within delegated authority

### 4.4 Data criticality

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Approving critical data elements
- Confirming why an element is considered critical
- Ensuring that appropriate controls are assigned
- Reviewing the critical data-element inventory
- Removing or reclassifying elements when their importance changes

### 4.5 Appropriate data use

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Defining approved business purposes
- Approving material new uses of domain data
- Ensuring that data consumers understand relevant restrictions
- Supporting review of sensitive or high-risk usage
- Participating in data-readiness assessments for analytics and AI

### 4.6 Data risk

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Understanding the principal risks affecting domain data
- Reviewing material incidents and control failures
- Approving domain remediation priorities
- Accepting residual risk within delegated authority
- Escalating risk beyond delegated authority

### 4.7 Governance performance

The Data Owner is accountable for:

- Reviewing governance KPIs
- Reviewing open and overdue issues
- Monitoring remediation progress
- Ensuring that governance forums operate effectively
- Supporting improvement in domain maturity
- Reporting material concerns to the Data Governance Council

## 5. Responsibilities

The Data Owner is responsible for ensuring that the following activities occur.

### Governance framework

- Apply enterprise Data Governance principles
- Ensure that domain practices align with approved standards
- Support implementation of governance controls
- Review domain maturity
- Sponsor governance improvements

### Ownership and stewardship

- Nominate appropriate Data Stewards
- Confirm stewardship responsibilities
- Ensure sufficient operational capacity
- Review Data Steward performance
- Resolve accountability gaps

### Business glossary

- Approve material business definitions
- Confirm term ownership
- Resolve conflicting interpretations
- Review retired or superseded definitions
- Support adoption of approved terminology

### Critical data elements

- Approve critical data elements
- Confirm quality and control requirements
- Review criticality periodically
- Ensure that ownership and stewardship are assigned
- Confirm material changes

### Data quality

- Approve quality thresholds
- Review data-quality scorecards
- Challenge persistent failures
- Approve remediation priorities
- Confirm closure of material issues

### Data classification and access

- Confirm business sensitivity and criticality
- Approve access according to delegated authority
- Support periodic access reviews
- Review material exceptions
- Confirm appropriate-use conditions

### Data issues

- Review high-severity issues
- Approve remediation plans
- Assign business priorities
- Escalate overdue or unresolved matters
- Approve closure of material issues
- Accept residual risk where authorised

### Governance exceptions

- Review exception requests
- Assess the business rationale
- Confirm compensating controls
- Approve low-risk exceptions within delegated authority
- Escalate material exceptions
- Ensure that exceptions are time-limited

### Analytics and AI

- Confirm ownership of data used in analytics and AI
- Review material data-quality limitations
- Approve use within delegated authority
- Participate in AI data-readiness assessments
- Ensure that limitations and risks are documented
- Support appropriate human oversight

---

## 6. Decision Rights

The Data Owner may approve:

- Domain business definitions
- Critical data elements
- Domain data-quality rules
- Domain quality thresholds
- Domain remediation priorities
- Standard data-access decisions within delegated authority
- Closure of material domain issues
- Low-risk governance exceptions
- Residual domain risk within delegated authority
- Domain participation in approved analytics or AI use cases

The Data Owner must escalate:

- Cross-domain ownership disputes
- Cross-domain definition conflicts
- Material enterprise-standard exceptions
- Risks exceeding delegated authority
- Material regulatory or customer impact
- Significant investment requirements
- Persistent unresolved high-severity issues
- Material data-related AI risks

The Data Owner works closely with:

| Stakeholder | Nature of relationship |

| Data Steward | Operational governance coordination |

| Technology teams | System and process implementation |
| Data consumers | Appropriate and informed data usage |

---

## 8. Domain Governance Forum Responsibilities

The Data Owner chairs or sponsors the Domain Governance Forum.

The Data Owner ensures that the forum:

- Meets according to the agreed cadence
- Reviews governance KPIs
- Reviews open data issues
- Tracks remediation actions
- Reviews business definitions
- Reviews critical data elements
- Assesses material changes
- Records decisions
- Escalates matters where required

The Data Owner ensures that the forum produces sufficient governance evidence.

---

## 9. Required Competencies

A Data Owner should demonstrate:

- Senior business judgement
- Understanding of the assigned data domain
- Governance and risk awareness
- Decision-making ability
- Stakeholder leadership
- Ability to resolve conflicting priorities
- Understanding of data quality
- Understanding of regulatory and control requirements
- Ability to sponsor remediation
- Clear communication
- Willingness to accept accountability

Technical expertise is helpful but is not the primary requirement.

---

The expected commitment depends on domain size, risk and maturity.

Typical activities include:

| Activity | Indicative frequency |
|---|---|
| Domain Governance Forum | Monthly |
| Governance KPI review | Monthly |
| High-severity issue review | Monthly or as required |
| Data Governance Council | Monthly |
| Critical data-element review | Quarterly |
| Data-quality threshold review | Quarterly |
| Access review | Quarterly or risk-based |
| Governance maturity review | Annual |
| Charter and standards review | Annual |
| Urgent issue escalation | As required |

---

## 11. Performance Measures

Data Owner effectiveness may be measured through:

- Percentage of critical data elements with approved ownership
- Percentage of critical data elements with approved definitions
- Percentage of domain quality rules meeting target
- Number of overdue high-severity issues
- Percentage of remediation actions completed within target
- Number of expired governance exceptions
- Percentage of access reviews completed on time
- Domain governance maturity score
- Timeliness of domain decisions
- Completion of assigned Council actions
- Stakeholder feedback

---

## 12. Evidence of Effective Ownership

Evidence may include:

- Approved business definitions
- Approved critical data-element records
- Data-quality threshold approvals
- Domain Governance Forum minutes
- Governance decisions
- Issue-remediation approvals
- Risk-acceptance records
- Access-review approvals
- Exception decisions
- AI data-readiness decisions
- Governance KPI reviews
- Maturity-improvement plans

---

## 13. Role Boundaries

The Data Owner does not:

- Perform all stewardship activities personally
- Operate technical platforms
- Replace Data Protection, Compliance or Information Security
- Delegate away final business accountability
- Approve matters beyond delegated authority
- Accept undocumented risk
- Determine technical implementation without specialist input
- Assume that technology teams own business data
- Approve data use without an identifiable business purpose
